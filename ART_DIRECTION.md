# The old kingdom

The visual target is an ancient magical kingdom with the craftsmanship and
cohesion of a premium adventure game. Functional geometry is a blockout, not
a finished asset. More primitives or a texture alone do not meet this bar.

The settlement stone is the foreground heart. Castle architecture rises in
the background, following a rocky ridge. Preserve open ground around the stone.
Buildings need believable foundations, accessible entrances, and a relationship
to slopes; terrain, collision, and rendered surfaces must agree.

Every addition should resolve silhouette and proportion, softened edges and
structural detail, correctly scaled materials, localized age and damage, and
its contribution to the larger composition. Decay should follow construction.
Avoid uniformly scattered props, repeated identical homes, stretched stone,
plain cone roofs, and arbitrary stepped cuts presented as finished ruins.

Use color deliberately: slate blue, emerald and ruby glass, faded purple and
teal heraldry, warm gold fittings, and restrained magical fire. Keep natural
areas quieter. Magic should belong to a place or ritual, not fill every space.

Review both a close view and the stone-facing overview. Verify walkability and
frame cost after meaningful changes. All external assets must remain CC0 or
public domain and be recorded in ASSETS.md; franchise references guide quality
and atmosphere, not asset provenance.

The palace is dedicated to Jupiter: use the ♃ glyph, imperial purple and gold
heraldry, and astronomical motifs. Limestone should retain material contrast;
avoid both uniformly muddy facades and overexposed white stone. Current Gothic
pieces are a custom procedural kit, not scanned production architecture.

The gatehouse is the first architectural detail study. Judge it from the road
at eye height as well as from the settlement stone: actual recessed openings,
legible stories and roof construction, a continuous walkable passage, and rock
that shares the terrain's height function. Expand this level of construction
across the remaining wings before adding more unrelated towers or ornament.
Keep fine detail subordinate to the silhouette, and weathering subordinate to
construction. Do not claim parity with a production AAA environment.

Ruined wall crowns must remain connected to their foundations; eroding a course
must never strand blocks above it. Remove decorative needle peaks from ruins.
Bridge entrances need load-bearing arches and abutments, not a thin floating ramp.
Root ivy at footings and ledges, branch around openings, and let moss collect at
damp edges while foot traffic keeps the main walking route worn clear.
